What This Module Covers

This module covers rate limiting and usage metering as they apply specifically to SaaS applications—the plan-based API limits, usage tracking, overage handling, and abuse prevention that protect your product from both intentional attacks and unintentional overuse by customers who don't realize they're hammering your API. You already know rate limiting fundamentals from the CADE program. This goes deeper into SaaS-specific patterns: different rate limits per plan tier, usage dashboards that show customers how close they are to their limits, grace periods for occasional spikes, and the billing integration that charges customers for overages or prompts them to upgrade.

Why It Matters

In SaaS, rate limiting isn't just about protecting your server—it's about monetization. Your Free plan gets 100 API calls per day. Your Pro plan gets 10,000. Your Enterprise plan gets 100,000. Rate limiting IS your pricing model. If your rate limiter doesn't work, free users consume as much as paying users and you have no reason to upgrade. Usage metering feeds your billing system. If you charge per API call, per active user, or per GB of storage, your metering must be accurate. Undercounting means lost revenue. Overcounting means angry customers and disputes.

What You Need to Know

Plan-based rate limits: Different subscription plans get different limits. Your rate limiter needs to know which plan the requesting user is on and apply the correct threshold. This means your rate limiting layer must integrate with your subscription data.

Usage metering and counting: Every billable event must be counted accurately. This means atomic counters that don't lose events under load, timestamped records for billing period accuracy, and real-time dashboards so customers can see their usage.

Overage handling strategies: When a customer hits their limit, you have options: hard block (stop working), soft limit (allow with warning), overage billing (charge per additional unit), or upgrade prompt (suggest the next plan). The right choice depends on your business model.

Abuse prevention vs. legitimate spikes: A customer who sends 10x their normal traffic for 5 minutes might be running a legitimate batch job—not attacking your system. Your rate limiter should distinguish between sustained abuse and temporary spikes, with grace periods for the latter.

Customer-facing usage dashboards: Customers need to see how much of their allocation they've used. A usage dashboard with current period consumption, historical trends, and approaching-limit warnings reduces support tickets and drives upgrades.

Your Toolkit

AI coding tool (pick one): Cursor, Lovable, Bolt, Claude Code—describe your rate limiting logic, usage tracking, and overage handling to it.

Rate limiting middleware: Upstash, Redis-based limiters, or built-in framework middleware. These handle the counting and enforcement at the request level.

Usage tracking database: A dedicated table or time-series database for recording usage events. This feeds both your customer dashboard and your billing integration.

Load testing tool: k6, Artillery, or simple curl scripts to simulate high traffic and verify your rate limiter actually blocks requests at the configured threshold.

Common Pitfalls

These are the mistakes vibecoders make most often in this area. No judgment—they're easy to make. But if you recognize any of them in your own workflow, fix them before sitting for the exam. Applying the same rate limit to all plans. Your free and paid customers should not have the same API limits. Rate limiting IS your pricing differentiation. Not showing customers their usage. If a customer doesn't know they're at 80% of their limit, they can't plan ahead. They'll hit the wall unexpectedly and blame you. Hard-blocking paying customers without warning. A paying customer who hits their limit should get a warning and a grace period—not an immediate 429 error. Reserve hard blocks for abuse and free tier enforcement. Counting usage at the wrong layer. If you count API calls at the gateway but your metered billing counts at the application layer, the numbers won't match and customers will dispute their bills. Not including rate limit headers in API responses. Clients need X-RateLimit-Remaining and X-RateLimit-Reset so they can throttle themselves before hitting the wall. Testing rate limits with one request at a time. Your rate limiter works at low traffic. Does it work when 50 concurrent requests arrive in the same second?

Self-Assessment Checklist

Before you take the exam, run through these questions. Every "no" is something to work on. Do different subscription plans have different rate limits enforced at the API level? Can customers see their current usage and remaining allocation in real time? Does hitting a limit produce a clear error message with upgrade guidance—not a generic server error? Are usage counters accurate under concurrent load? Do API responses include rate limit headers (remaining quota, reset time)? Is there a grace period for temporary spikes before hard-blocking a paying customer? Does usage data feed accurately into your billing system for metered pricing?
