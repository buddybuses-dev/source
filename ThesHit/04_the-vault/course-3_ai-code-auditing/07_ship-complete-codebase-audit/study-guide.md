## What You Need to Know

**1. Scoping: what is in, what is out, time per layer.** Every audit begins with boundaries. What systems are included, what is explicitly out of scope, and how much time each layer gets given the engagement size. A $200 rapid audit budgets minutes per layer and leans heavily on the automated pass; a comprehensive engagement budgets deep manual review. Scoping protects both sides: the client knows what they are getting, and you avoid the trap of unbounded work. An unscoped audit expands until it is unprofitable and still feels incomplete.

**2. The audit workflow, assembled.** The full sequence: automated pass first (Module 3) to establish the mechanical floor fast, then the manual pass (Module 4) targeting the hotspots automation cannot see, then consolidation, merging results, deduplicating, and resolving conflicts between tools and observations, then severity assignment (Module 5) across the unified findings, then report generation with the scorecard. Running automated before manual is deliberate: the scan results inform where the manual attention is most needed, and the manual pass interprets what the scan flagged.

**3. Client communication during the audit.** Silence during an engagement breeds anxiety. Professional auditing means setting expectations at the start, kickoff, what you will need, when they will hear from you, and flagging anything genuinely urgent immediately rather than saving it for the report. If the automated pass surfaces an actively exploitable Critical, the client hears about that one now, with initial guidance, not in a document three days later. Communication cadence is part of the deliverable, not overhead around it.

**4. Delivering findings without panic.** The delivery conversation sets whether the client acts wisely or badly. You lead with posture and the plan, not a body count. You frame findings as fixable and expected, vibebuilt apps have these patterns; that is why the audit exists. You pair every serious finding with its remediation and effort so fear never arrives without a path. The goal of delivery is a client who understands their risk, trusts your assessment, and is ready to act, not a client who is scared into paralysis or into rushing bad fixes.

**5. What an audit is and is not.** Honesty about limits is a professional obligation and a trust builder. An audit is point-in-time: it reflects the system as it was during the audit window, not a guarantee about tomorrow's code. It is not monitoring; it does not watch for new vulnerabilities after delivery. It is not a certificate of unbreakability; it is a serious, structured examination that finds what a skilled reviewer directing good tools can find in the scoped time. Overselling an audit as permanent safety is the fastest way to lose a client when something surfaces later. Stating the limits plainly is what makes the recurring relationship make sense.

**6. Positioning audits as a recurring relationship.** The point-in-time nature of an audit is not a weakness to hide; it is the reason the relationship continues. Code changes, new features ship, new patterns get generated, and the posture drifts. The $200 rapid audit is the low-friction entry point that proves value; the scorecard becomes the baseline; the next audit measures movement; and comprehensive engagements or ongoing retainers follow for clients whose risk justifies them. You position from the first engagement: this is a snapshot, here is your baseline, security is a practice not an event, and here is how we keep the score green as you grow. That framing turns a $200 transaction into a durable client.

## Common Pitfalls

- Starting an audit with no scope and watching it expand past profitability
- Running manual review blind instead of letting the automated pass direct attention
- Going silent for the whole engagement and surfacing a Critical only at delivery
- Delivering a raw fear-inducing findings dump with no posture framing or plan
- Overselling the audit as permanent safety and getting burned when new code breaks
- Treating the audit as a one-off transaction and leaving the recurring relationship on the table

## Self-Assessment Checklist

- Can I scope an audit's boundaries and per-layer time to a stated budget?
- Do I run the workflow in order, letting automated results steer the manual pass?
- Do I escalate an actively exploitable Critical the moment I find it?
- Does my delivery lead with posture and plan rather than a finding count?
- Can I state plainly what this audit does and does not promise?
- Can I position the rapid audit as the first step in an ongoing security relationship?
