## Testing and Refining Your Position

> "Direct AI to track positioning signals for 90 days while you resist the urge to flinch at week three."

## Why This Matters

You chose a position, built an offer, and wrote the sentence. None of that makes it correct; it makes it testable. Positioning is a hypothesis until clients validate it with behavior, and the builders who win are the ones who read the signals honestly instead of quitting early or committing blindly.

## Core Concepts

**Positioning is a hypothesis.** You made a claim: this audience, with this problem, will choose me for this reason. The market grades the claim through behavior: who reaches out, what they say, what they compare you against. Your job in this phase is to collect the grades, not to defend the thesis.

**Working signals.** Three behaviors say the position is landing. Inbound inquiries mention your specialty: "I saw you build intake systems for clinics." Referrals describe you accurately: your sentence traveled intact through a third party. Prospects self-qualify: "we're a clinic, so this is exactly what we need," before you have explained anything. All three mean the market can find, remember, and repeat you.

**Failing signals.** Three behaviors say it is not landing. You keep explaining what you do, even to people who found you on purpose. Prospects compare you to generalists and Fiverr listings, meaning your specialty never registered. You keep competing on price, meaning nothing but the number differentiates you. These are signals about the position or its distribution, not about your worth.

**Adjust versus commit.** The failure signals have two very different causes. Distribution failure: the position is fine but almost nobody has encountered it yet; the fix is more reach, not new words. Position failure: people encounter it clearly and still respond with confusion or price-shopping; the fix is revising the audience, the problem, or the language. Diagnose which one you have before changing anything, because rewriting a position nobody saw teaches you nothing.

**The 90-day test.** Commit to one position, unchanged, for 90 days of consistent visible effort: sentence on every surface, weekly niche content, steady outreach. Track the signals monthly. Ninety days is long enough for referral loops to complete and short enough to bound the risk. Changing course at week three because of silence is not data-driven; silence at week three is the expected value.

## How It Works

Run it like an experiment. Day one: lock the positioning sentence on every surface and log your baseline (inbound per month, referral accuracy, price objections per deal). Every week: publish niche content and do a fixed amount of outreach, so the position actually gets encountered. Every month: direct AI to score the month's inquiries and conversations against the six signals. Day 90: read the trend, not the anecdotes. Working signals trending up: commit harder, raise prices. Failing signals with weak distribution: extend 30 to 60 days with more reach. Failing signals despite real distribution: revise one variable (audience, problem, or language) and start a fresh 90 days. One variable, or you cannot tell what fixed it.

## Directing AI

1. "Build me a positioning signal tracker: the three working signals and three failing signals, with a place to log each inquiry, referral, and sales conversation."
2. "Here are this month's inquiries and call notes. Classify each against the six signals and summarize the trend versus last month."
3. "Analyze my last ten leads: did they mention my specialty, compare me to generalists, or negotiate price first? Give me the counts."
4. "My signals are failing. Here are my traffic and outreach numbers. Diagnose whether this looks like a distribution failure or a position failure, and say why."
5. "Draft the revised positioning sentence changing only the audience, keeping problem and language identical, for the next 90-day test."

## Common Mistakes

1. Abandoning the position at week three because inbound is quiet, before referral loops could possibly complete.
2. Changing audience, problem, and language all at once, so the next test cannot attribute the result.
3. Reading one enthusiastic conversation as validation while ignoring ten price-shopping ones.
4. Diagnosing position failure when the real problem is that nobody has encountered the position yet.
5. Tracking feelings instead of signals, so month three is judged by mood instead of counts.
6. Treating failing signals as personal failure instead of information about a testable hypothesis.

## Real-World Application

A builder positions as "automated quoting systems for landscapers" and by week four wants to quit: two inquiries, both price-shoppers. Instead of flinching he runs the diagnosis with AI and finds a distribution problem: his content reached almost no landscapers, and his outreach totaled nine emails. He doubles outreach and starts answering questions in two landscaper Facebook groups. By day 75 the signals flip: an inquiry opens with "you're the quoting guy," and a referral arrives describing his service almost word for word. Day 90 review: five inbound, three mentioned the specialty, price objections down by half. He commits, raises his price 30 percent, and the next quarter's test is about the offer, not the position. The position was never broken; it was invisible.

## Decision Framework

- Week three and silent? Expected. Keep distribution running; judge at the monthly marks, not daily.
- Inquiries mention your specialty? Working signal; hold course and consider raising prices.
- Still explaining what you do to warm leads? Language problem; sharpen the sentence, keep the niche.
- Compared to Fiverr in every deal? Specialty is not registering; check distribution first, then position.
- Failing signals but tiny reach? Distribution failure; extend the test with more visibility.
- Failing signals despite real reach? Position failure; revise one variable and restart the clock.

## Tool and Platform Notes

The tracker is a spreadsheet or Notion table: date, source, what they said, which signal it maps to. Direct AI to do the monthly classification and trend summary from your raw notes. Your email, LinkedIn analytics, and website stats are enough to measure distribution honestly. No analytics suite required; the discipline is monthly review, not fancier dashboards.

## Key Takeaways

- Positioning is a hypothesis; client behavior is the grade.
- Working signals: specialty-mentioning inbound, accurate referrals, self-qualifying prospects.
- Failing signals: constant explaining, generalist comparisons, price competition.
- Diagnose distribution failure versus position failure before changing anything.
- Run 90 days, change one variable per cycle, and judge trends, not anecdotes.
