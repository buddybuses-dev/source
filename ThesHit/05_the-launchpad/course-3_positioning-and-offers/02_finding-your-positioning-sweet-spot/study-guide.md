## Finding Your Positioning Sweet Spot

> "Direct AI to map skills, demand, and competitors while you judge where the three circles truly overlap."

## Why This Matters

Module 1 convinced you to get specific. This module is where you choose what to get specific about, and choosing badly costs months. The sweet spot is not a vibe; it is an intersection you can locate with evidence you mostly already have, including the validation work you finished in the last course.

## Core Concepts

**The three-circle intersection.** Your position lives where three things overlap: what you can build (skills), what people will pay for (demand), and what you actually enjoy (sustainability). Two out of three fails predictably. Skills plus demand without enjoyment burns you out by month six. Skills plus enjoyment without demand is a hobby. Demand plus enjoyment without skills is a promise you cannot keep yet.

**Your validation evidence is the demand circle.** The previous course left you with real signals: who responded, who paid or preordered, which problem statements made people lean in. Do not start the demand analysis from scratch; start from who already said yes and ask what they have in common.

**Three positioning axes.** Industry positioning: "I serve dental practices." Problem positioning: "I fix no-show appointment losses." Platform positioning: "I build on Airtable." Industry buys you insider trust, problem buys you urgency, platform buys you ecosystem demand but chains you to someone else's roadmap. You can combine two, and industry plus problem is the strongest pairing for most builders.

**Positioning statements are testable.** "I build [what] for [whom] so they can [outcome]" is a hypothesis. You test it by saying it to real prospects and watching what happens: do they ask a follow-up question about their own situation, or do they nod politely and change the subject? Leaning in is data. Polite nodding is data too.

**Too broad, too narrow, just right.** Too broad: prospects still ask what exactly you do, and you still get compared to generalists. Too narrow: the audience is so small you cannot find enough of them to sustain a pipeline, or they all know each other and one bad project ends you. Just right: prospects instantly self-recognize, and there are enough of them to lose a few.

## How It Works

Run the intersection as an exercise, not a meditation. List every system you have actually shipped (skills). Pull the validation evidence and list who responded with money or strong intent (demand). Write down which projects you would happily repeat and which you dreaded (sustainability). Direct AI to cross-reference the three lists and propose candidate positions on each axis. Then draft three positioning statements and test them in ten real conversations before choosing. The test is behavioral: follow-up questions and referrals within two weeks mean signal; silence means keep looking.

## Directing AI

1. "Here are my shipped projects, my validation results, and my honest notes on what I enjoyed. Cross-reference them and propose five candidate positions."
2. "For each candidate position, estimate audience size and list three places those buyers gather online where I could verify demand."
3. "Write my positioning statement three ways: industry-led, problem-led, and platform-led. Same underlying service in each."
4. "Draft 10 short outreach messages I can send to past contacts to test this positioning statement in real conversations."
5. "Here are notes from my ten test conversations. Score the reactions from lean-in to polite nod and tell me what pattern you see."

## Common Mistakes

1. Choosing by market size alone, landing on a lucrative niche you have no skills for and no interest in.
2. Ignoring the validation evidence and picking a niche based on what sounds impressive at meetups.
3. Treating enjoyment as optional, then wondering why the successful niche feels like a prison by month six.
4. Testing positioning by asking friends if they like it instead of watching how real prospects react.
5. Going so narrow that the entire addressable market is forty people who all talk to each other.
6. Platform positioning without a backup plan, so one vendor pivot erases the entire practice.

## Real-World Application

A builder finishes validation with paid interest from three property managers, two dentists, and a gym owner. He nearly picks dentistry because a podcast said it was lucrative, but his notes say the dental conversations drained him and the property management ones ran long because he was having fun. His shipped work includes two maintenance-ticketing tools, which is real skill overlap. He drafts "I build maintenance request systems for small property managers" and tests it in ten conversations. Seven produce follow-up questions about their own buildings; one produces a referral on the spot. The dentist version of the statement, tested in parallel, produces polite nods. He commits to property management, and the decision took two weeks instead of six months because it ran on evidence.

## Decision Framework

- Two circles overlap but not the third? Keep looking; each missing circle has a predictable failure mode.
- Multiple viable niches? Weight the one your validation evidence already funded with real interest.
- Industry, problem, or platform? Industry plus problem is strongest; platform only with an exit plan.
- Statement getting polite nods in tests? That is a no; revise the audience or the problem, not the font.
- Niche feels too small? Count reachable buyers; if you cannot lose a few clients safely, widen one notch.
- Enjoyment hard to judge? Read your own project notes: what you delayed starting tells the truth.

## Tool and Platform Notes

Your validation artifacts from the prior course are the primary dataset; keep them open through this whole module. Direct AI to scan niche communities (industry subreddits, Facebook groups, trade association sites) to size demand and find where buyers gather. LinkedIn search gives rough counts of your target role. A spreadsheet of ten test conversations with reaction scores beats any market research report you could buy.

## Key Takeaways

- The sweet spot is the overlap of skills, demand, and enjoyment; two out of three fails predictably.
- Your validation evidence is a head start on the demand circle; use it instead of guessing.
- Industry, problem, and platform are different axes; industry plus problem suits most builders best.
- Positioning statements are hypotheses; test them in real conversations and score the reactions.
- Just right means prospects self-recognize instantly and the audience is big enough to lose a few.
