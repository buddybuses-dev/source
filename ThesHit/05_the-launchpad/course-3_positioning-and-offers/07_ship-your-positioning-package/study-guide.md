## Ship: Your Positioning Package

> "Direct AI to assemble, polish, and stress-test the five-part positioning package you will run your business on."

## Why This Matters

Everything downstream runs on what you produce here. Pricing Your Builds prices this offer. The Discovery Call sells this position. Proposals and SOWs formalize this promise. If the package is clear, everything downstream gets easier; if it is vague, every future conversation becomes a renegotiation of who you are.

## Core Concepts

**The five-part package.** One document with five components. Positioning statement: who you serve and what you build, from Module 2's sweet spot. Offer description: the transformation with deliverable, timeline, price, guarantee, and bonuses, from Module 3. Value proposition: the one sentence in client language that passes the so-what test, from Module 4. Competitive differentiators: the levers you pull and why a prospect picks you, from Module 5. Proof plan: the evidence you will build over the next 90 days, from Module 5's proof engine pointed forward.

**The proof plan is a commitment, not a wish.** It names specific artifacts with dates: which past project becomes a case study by when, which clients get testimonial requests this week, what content ships weekly and where, and which numbers you will start capturing on every project so future proof has data. Ninety days from now the plan should have produced a visible portfolio, not intentions.

**Coherence is the quality bar.** The five parts must tell one story. The value proposition promises what the offer delivers; the differentiators explain why the positioning statement is credible; the proof plan generates evidence for exactly those differentiators. The classic capstone failure is five decent parts that describe five slightly different businesses.

**The package is a working document.** It feeds directly into downstream courses: the offer's price is the starting point for Pricing Your Builds, the value proposition opens your discovery calls, the differentiators arm you for objection handling, and the whole package templates your proposals. Vague inputs here become fights later; a discovery call cannot rescue a position you never defined.

**Shipped beats perfect.** The package is a 90-day hypothesis with a test protocol from Module 6 attached, not a constitution. Builders who polish for weeks are avoiding the market's grade. Ship it, run the test, revise from signals.

## How It Works

Assemble from existing parts; this module is integration, not invention. Pull your Module 2 positioning choice, Module 3 offer stack, Module 4 winning sentence, and Module 5 lever assessment into one document. Direct AI to draft the assembly, then run the coherence pass: does each part reference the same audience, the same problem, the same promise? Fix mismatches by changing the outlier, not by loosening everything. Then write the proof plan with names and dates. Finally, run the stress test: have AI role-play a skeptical prospect armed with your package, and check that every question it raises is answered somewhere in the five parts. Ship when coherent, not when perfect.

## Directing AI

1. "Assemble my positioning statement, offer stack, value proposition, and differentiators into one package document, flagging any place they disagree about audience, problem, or promise."
2. "Run a coherence check: list every claim in the package and which component backs it, and flag claims no other component supports."
3. "Draft my 90-day proof plan: one case study from these project notes with a completion date, testimonial requests for these three clients, and a weekly content schedule with topics."
4. "Role-play a skeptical prospect who has read my package. Ask the ten hardest questions, then show me where each answer lives, or does not, in the five parts."
5. "Condense the finished package into a one-page version I can paste into a proposal and read aloud on a discovery call."

## Common Mistakes

1. Writing five components that quietly describe five different businesses and calling it done.
2. Making the proof plan a list of intentions with no artifact names and no dates.
3. Polishing for weeks instead of shipping the 90-day hypothesis and collecting real signals.
4. Inventing new positioning during assembly instead of integrating what six modules already produced.
5. Skipping the stress test, so the first hard question arrives live on a discovery call.
6. Filing the package away after shipping instead of feeding it into pricing, calls, and proposals.

## Real-World Application

A builder finishes the capstone in two evenings because the parts already exist: her sweet spot is inventory systems for independent pharmacies, her offer is a fixed-price four-week install with a guarantee, her sentence survived testing in Module 4, and her levers are industry knowledge plus speed. The coherence pass catches one mismatch: her differentiators brag about speed, but her offer copy says "timeline flexible," and she fixes the offer to match. Her proof plan names its dates: one pharmacy case study written by Friday, two testimonial requests sent Monday, one post per week in a pharmacy owners' group. Three weeks later, her first discovery call in the new niche takes twenty minutes, because every question the pharmacist asks is already answered in the package she reads from. The next course prices exactly this offer; nothing downstream has to guess.

## Decision Framework

- Components disagree about the audience? Fix the outlier component; do not widen the audience to fit all five.
- Proof plan has no dates? It is a wish; assign artifacts to calendar weeks before shipping.
- Package feels unfinished? Check coherence and the stress test; if both pass, ship and let the market grade it.
- Stress-test question has no answer in the package? Add the answer to the weakest component, not a footnote.
- Downstream course asks for an input? Pull it from the package verbatim; rewriting per course breaks coherence.
- Signals from the 90-day test come back? Revise the package one variable at a time, as Module 6 taught.

## Tool and Platform Notes

One document is the deliverable: Google Docs or Notion, with the one-page condensed version alongside it. Direct AI to handle assembly, coherence checking, and the prospect role-play. Calendar the proof plan dates immediately in whatever task system you actually check. Keep the package where you will reopen it, because Pricing Your Builds, The Discovery Call, and Proposals and SOWs all start by asking for it.

## Key Takeaways

- The package has five parts: positioning statement, offer, value proposition, differentiators, proof plan.
- Coherence is the bar: five parts, one story, one audience, one promise.
- The proof plan commits to named artifacts with dates, producing a visible portfolio in 90 days.
- Ship the coherent package and test it; polishing past coherence is market avoidance.
- Everything downstream (pricing, discovery calls, proposals) runs on this document.
