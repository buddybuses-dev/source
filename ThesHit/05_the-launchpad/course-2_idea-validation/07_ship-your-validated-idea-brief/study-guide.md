## What This Module Covers

This is the capstone. Everything from Modules 1 through 6 converges into one deliverable: a one-page Validated Idea Brief covering the problem, the evidence, the solution concept, the target buyer, the market size estimate, your competitive position, and your first build plan.

The brief is the receipt for your validation work. The builder with the CRM for dentists idea should be able to hand this page to anyone in The Faction and have them understand exactly what problem dental offices have, who said so, what the solution looks like, and why it can win. If you cannot fill out a section with confidence, that is not a formatting problem. That is a signal to go back and validate more.

This brief becomes the source of truth for everything downstream in the Launchpad: positioning, pricing, proposals, and client acquisition all pull directly from it.

## Why It Matters

I have watched hundreds of builders skip this step. They finish their research, feel good, and jump straight into directing their AI to build. Three weeks later they cannot remember which insights were real evidence and which were enthusiasm. The vibebuilder with the mobile app idea who "talked to some people" but wrote nothing down is now making product decisions from memory, and memory always flatters the idea.

The brief forces honesty. Writing "12 of 15 dentists said no-shows cost them $200 or more per week" is a very different sentence than "dentists hate no-shows." One is evidence, the other is a vibe. When your idea lives on one page with its evidence attached, weak spots become visible before they become expensive, and when it is time to pitch, price, or brief your AI, you are working from documented proof instead of hope.

## What You Need to Know

### The One-Page Discipline

The brief is one page on purpose. If you need five pages to explain your idea, you do not understand it well enough yet. One page forces you to keep only the strongest evidence and clearest statements, which is exactly what clients, partners, and your AI need.

### Evidence Over Opinion

Every claim must trace back to something real: an interview quote, a landing page conversion rate, a waitlist number, a competitor's pricing page. M2 and M3 taught you the difference between "people said they liked it" and "people gave me their email, time, or money." Only the second kind belongs in the brief.

### The Problem and Buyer Sections

State the problem in the buyer's own words, then name the specific person who has it. Not "dental practices" but "office managers at 2 to 5 dentist practices who track recalls in spreadsheets." The narrower your buyer definition, the more useful every downstream module becomes.

### Market Size and Competitive Position

The brief carries a defensible bottom-up market estimate from M4 with your math shown, plus a one-line competitive position from M5: who else solves this, and why your angle wins anyway. "No competitors" is not a position. It is usually a warning that there is no market.

### The First Build Plan

The brief ends with what you will build first: the smallest version that tests your riskiest remaining assumption, a rough timeline, and the success metric that proves it worked. This is what you hand your AI when you start prompting. Scope it in weeks, not months.

### The Confidence Test

The final gate from M6: read your finished brief out loud. Any section that makes you wince, hedge, or reach for "probably" is built on assumption instead of evidence. Go back to the relevant module and close the gap before you build.

- **The Evidence Ledger**: A running list of every piece of proof you gathered across M2 through M5, with source and date. Pull the strongest three to five items into the brief and keep the rest as backup.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Writing the brief from memory**: If you did not capture evidence as you went, you will reconstruct it optimistically. The brief becomes fiction with formatting.
- **Padding weak sections with words**: A vague section written confidently is worse than a blank one, because it hides the gap you most need to close.
- **Skipping the market math**: Dropping in "$5B industry" from a headline means your pricing and positioning inherit a fantasy number.
- **Treating the brief as a formality**: Rush it and you will prompt your AI with no clear buyer, no metric, and no scope. Three months later you have a product nobody asked for.
- **Hiding contradictory evidence**: A brief with only flattering data is a sales pitch to yourself.
- **Never updating it**: When new evidence lands, update the page. A stale brief quietly misleads every downstream decision.

- I can define my target buyer specifically enough that a stranger could find ten of them
- I can show the bottom-up math behind my market size estimate
- I can state in one line who else solves this problem and why my angle wins
- I can describe my first build by the riskiest assumption it tests and the metric that proves it
- I can identify which module to revisit when any section of my brief feels weak

## AI Audit Prompt Template

Once your brief is drafted, paste it into this prompt and let your AI stress-test it before you build.

`You are a skeptical startup advisor reviewing my one-page Validated Idea Brief. Find gaps and weak evidence. Do not encourage me. Here is my brief: [PASTE YOUR FULL VALIDATED IDEA BRIEF HERE] Audit each section (problem, evidence, solution, buyer, market size, competitive position, build plan): 1. Rate the evidence: STRONG (payments, signups, repeated interview patterns), WEAK (opinions, compliments, hypotheticals), or MISSING. 2. Flag every claim with no source, quote, or number behind it. 3. Flag vague language: "many," "huge market," "everyone," "probably," or any buyer definition a stranger could not use to find ten real people. 4. Check my market math for top-down shortcuts or unstated assumptions. 5. Check whether my build plan tests my riskiest assumption and has a measurable success metric. Then give me the three weakest points, my single riskiest remaining assumption, and one validation action per weak point I could complete this week. Be blunt.`
