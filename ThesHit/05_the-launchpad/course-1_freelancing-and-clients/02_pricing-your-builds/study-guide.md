# Module 2: Pricing Your Builds — Study Guide

## What This Module Covers

This module covers how to price AI-directed builds: modular pricing, project pricing, retainers, and the judgment calls between hourly, fixed, and value-based structures. Pricing is where most new builders quietly sabotage themselves. They price off their hours, discount out of fear, and end up earning less than they did as employees while carrying all the risk of a business owner.

Here's the shift you have to make: your speed is the product. When you direct AI to finish in hours what used to take weeks, hourly billing punishes you for being good. The builders who thrive price against the outcome's worth to the client, sell defined blocks of scope at set prices, and put every term in writing before work starts.

You'll direct AI through all of it. AI models the value of a build to a specific client, generates tiered proposal options, drafts the scope language, and builds your invoicing and payment infrastructure. Your job is to set the floor, pick the number, and hold it in the room. AI does the math and the paperwork. You do the judgment.

## Why It Matters

Pricing is where underconfidence becomes permanent. Price your first project too low and that number follows you: the client anchors on it, referrals arrive expecting it, and raising rates later feels like a betrayal. Worse, a price far below every alternative doesn't read as a bargain to buyers. It reads as risk. Underpricing loses deals you were trying to win with it.

AI tools are excellent at modeling what a build saves a client, generating pricing scenarios, drafting proposals, and standing up payment systems in an afternoon. They're terrible at deciding what you're worth, holding a number under pressure, and knowing when a discount request deserves a trade instead of a yes. Those decisions are yours. This module teaches you what to direct, what to charge, and where your floor is.

## Module Certification Goal

You can direct AI to model the business value of a build and generate tiered pricing options, structure engagements with written scope, deposits, and milestone payments, and defend your price in negotiation by trading rather than discounting.

## What You Need to Know

- **Anchor on value, and direct AI to quantify it:** If a build saves a client $150,000 a year in labor, the price should be anchored on that outcome, not on the hours it takes you to produce. Before every proposal, direct AI to model the value: hours saved times labor cost, revenue unlocked, errors eliminated. Feed it what you learned in discovery and have it produce the value case in the client's own numbers. When your price sits next to a quantified outcome, it looks small. Alone, it looks big.
- **Modular pricing sells certainty, not time:** Modular pricing means selling fixed blocks of defined scope at a set price, so clients buy predictable units of delivery. A reference structure in this industry prices a defined engineering module at $8,000 for a 40-hour block. The number matters less than the shape: the client buys a known outcome at a known price, and you sell defined capacity instead of open-ended time. Direct AI to draft your module menu: what a block includes, what it excludes, and what it costs.
- **Written scope is your fixed-price armor:** A fixed price without written scope is a blank check the client holds. Your scope document states what's included and, just as explicitly, what is not. Direct AI to draft the scope section of every proposal, then have it generate the "out of scope" list by brainstorming everything a client might assume is included. When the third "one more small feature" arrives mid-project, you welcome it and route it through change control: new scope, priced and scheduled as an addition.
- **Three options beat one number:** A proposal with two or three scoped options shifts the client's decision from yes-or-no to which-one, and lets them self-select their budget. Present the most complete option first. It anchors the conversation at full value and makes the middle option feel reasonable. Direct AI to generate the tiers from your discovery notes: a starter scope, the recommended build, and the full system, each with its own price and outcome.
- **Deposits and milestones protect your cash:** Require a deposit before starting any first-time client's build. It confirms commitment, funds your early work, and filters out clients who never intended to pay. Structure the rest against milestones: payments as defined deliverables ship, a final on handoff. Direct AI to build the infrastructure: Stripe payment links, invoice templates with amounts and due dates, and a payment schedule that drops straight into your proposal.
- **Discounts get traded, never given, and you set your floor first:** When a prospect asks for 20% off, the disciplined move is a trade: reduced scope, a case study, a referral, prepayment, or a longer term. Never a quiet yes, and never an inflated makeup invoice later. Before any negotiation, direct AI to help you calculate your walk-away number: the floor below which the deal stops being worth doing, including your tools, taxes, unbillable time, and margin. Decide it calmly in advance. Never under pressure in the room.

## Your Toolkit

- **Claude or ChatGPT:** Your pricing analyst and proposal writer. Value modeling, tiered options, scope drafts, negotiation prep, and walk-away math all run through it.
- **Stripe:** Payment links, deposits, and milestone invoicing. Direct AI to walk you through the setup so a client can pay you the same day they say yes.
- **QuickBooks or Wave:** Invoicing and the money records behind your pricing. Your rates have to cover what the business actually costs, and this is where you see it.
- **Notion:** Your rate card, module menu, and proposal templates live here so every quote starts from a standard instead of a blank page.

## Certification Exam Topics

Every exam question is scenario-based. You'll see a situation and need to identify what's right, what's wrong, or what to do next. Here's what gets tested:

- Can you define modular pricing and explain what fixed blocks of defined scope do for both the client and the builder?
- Can you identify why pure hourly billing undervalues AI-directed engineering specifically, and when hourly is still the right choice?
- Can you anchor a price on delivered value rather than hours or tool costs, and spot the cost-plus fallacy when a client raises it?
- Can you evaluate what protects a fixed-price quote, and how a written scope with explicit exclusions settles mid-project disputes?
- Can you route a mid-project feature request through change control instead of absorbing it silently?
- Can you structure payments on a multi-week build: deposit, milestones, and final on handoff, and explain what that protects?
- Can you respond to a discount request with a trade, present tiered options with the anchor first, and recognize what "that's too expensive" usually means?
- Can you identify a walk-away number, where the free scoping line sits, and how rate increases work in practice?

## Common Pitfalls

These are the mistakes vibecoders make most often at this stage. No judgment, they're easy to make. But if you recognize any of them in your own workflow, fix them before sitting for the exam.

- **You bill hourly for work AI helps you finish fast.** The better you get, the less you earn. That's backwards. Speed is your margin, not your discount. Sell the outcome at a fixed price and let your efficiency belong to you, not to the invoice.
- **You quote a number in the first five minutes.** A prospect demands a price before you understand the problem, and you blurt your standard rate to avoid seeming evasive. Now you're anchored to a number that has nothing to do with their scope. Share a typical range, then explain how discovery makes it exact.
- **You price off your tool costs.** A client hears your AI subscriptions cost a few hundred dollars a month and argues your price should reflect it. Price reflects the value and accountability you deliver, not the input costs of your tools. Nobody prices a surgeon off the cost of the scalpel.
- **You give discounts unconditionally.** Every unreciprocated discount teaches the client that your first number is fiction. If you choose to move, trade it for something real: smaller scope, a case study, prepayment, a longer term. The price can flex. The value of your work can't.
- **You skip the exclusions list.** Your scope says what's included and stays silent on everything else, so the client fills the silence with assumptions. "I thought that was included" is the most expensive sentence in service work. Direct AI to write the not-included list with the same care as the deliverables.
- **You take 100% on completion.** Weeks of delivered work, zero dollars collected, and one client mood swing between you and payment. That's not a payment structure, it's a donation with extra steps. Deposit up front, milestones as you ship, final on handoff. Every time.

## Self-Assessment Checklist

Before you take the exam, run through these questions. Every "no" is something to work on.

- Can you state your walk-away number for a typical engagement, calculated with tools, taxes, and unbillable time included?
- Have you directed AI to build a module menu with defined scope, explicit exclusions, and a set price per block?
- Do you know how you'd quantify the value of a build to a specific client, in their numbers?
- Does your proposal template present two or three scoped options with the most complete option first?
- Do you require a deposit from every first-time client, with milestone payments defined before work starts?
- Can you name three things you'd trade for a discount instead of just lowering the price?
- Do you know what you'd say when a prospect calls your price too expensive?

## AI Audit Prompt Template

Copy this prompt into your AI assistant to get a quick health check. It checks the same things the certification exam covers.

> Review my pricing setup and check the following. For each one, tell me pass or fail with a specific example: Value anchor: Here is my last proposal [paste it]. Is the price anchored to a quantified client outcome or to my hours and costs? Structure: Do I sell defined blocks of scope at fixed prices, with explicit inclusions and exclusions in writing? Options: Does my proposal present tiered options with the most complete option first, or a single take-it-or-leave-it number? Payment terms: Do my engagements start with a deposit and pay against milestones, or do I carry all the risk until completion? Floor: Based on these business costs [paste your tools, taxes, and monthly overhead], calculate my minimum viable price per module and flag any current pricing below it. Discount discipline: Here is how I handled my last discount request [describe it]. Did I trade or just concede? Give me an overall score out of 6 and list the top 3 things to fix first.
